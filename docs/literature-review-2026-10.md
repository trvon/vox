# Vox: literature and ecosystem review

> Generated by a gpt-6.1-sol subagent (pi) on 2026-10-27. Implementation status after follow-up: stateful anti-aliased resampling, archival capture, ordered dropped-audio accounting, OS-owned durable jobs, property/lifecycle tests, official sherpa-onnx 1.13.8, and cursor-based transcript tools are implemented. Parakeet v2/v3 selection and timestamp-based boundary ownership exist, but real-lecture accuracy/timing validation remains open. Diarization, negotiated MCP Tasks, and punctuation models remain research recommendations, not completed features.
**As of 2026-10-27. Read-only review; no inference benchmarks run.**

## Executive recommendation

**Build a durable recorder first, then add Parakeet TDT for lecture transcription.** Keep Moonshine for lightweight interactive use. Prefer VAD-guided, overlapping offline decoding over implementing streaming Whisper initially. Make transcripts timestamped, paginated artifacts; leave blog composition to the calling LLM.

The repository already exceeds its overview: `README.md` and `src/runtime.rs` contain background listening and an inbox. However:

- The inbox and pending audio are memory-only.
- `src/audio.rs` silently ignores `try_send` failures.
- `src/runtime.rs` concatenates speech without preserving source offsets.
- STT returns only text; inbox timestamps represent processing time, not speech position.
- Background inference tasks are not explicitly joined during shutdown.
- The hand-written calendar conversion in `chrono_now()` is inaccurate.

These are more immediate lecture-recording risks than model WER.

GPU leaderboard **RTFx is not CPU RTF**. The [Open ASR evaluation paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.06961) explains the reproducibility problem.

## B. Long-form decoding

Start with **VAD-guided offline chunks**, not one hours-long recognition call.

- Target roughly **15–30 seconds**; split at nearby silence, but enforce a maximum during uninterrupted speech.
- Preserve **200–300ms boundary padding** and **0.5–1s overlap** for forced splits.
- Decode overlap, reconcile timestamped/token hypotheses, and commit only the nonduplicated interior. Blind string concatenation loses or repeats words.
- Preserve original sample coordinates throughout. Removing silence must not collapse the recording timeline.
- Flush capture, resampler, VAD and inference queues on stop.

For live previews, [Whisper-Streaming](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.14743) uses **LocalAgreement**: commit the common prefix across successive hypotheses. It repeatedly decodes audio, increasing compute. [Simul-Whisper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.10052) uses attention-guided decoding and truncation detection; implementing its attention access through sherpa is additional work.

Silence filtering reduces hallucination but cannot prove correctness. Reject/flag repetition and implausible token rates; preserve suspicious audio. [WhisperX](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.00747) establishes VAD segmentation plus forced alignment as a useful long-form architecture. Alignment timestamps a hypothesis—it does not authenticate its words.

## C. VAD

[Silero v6](https://github.com/snakers4/silero-vad/releases) reports **16% fewer errors on noisy real-life data** and **11% fewer on multi-domain validation** than its predecessor. V6.2 adds edge-case robustness; latest package v6.2.3 mostly changes packaging. Pin the actual ONNX artifact/hash rather than treating the mutable `silero_vad.onnx` URL as a version.

[TEN VAD](https://github.com/TEN-framework/ten-vad) is integrated into sherpa and offers fine-grained frames and vendor-reported efficiency advantages. Its comparisons are not proof of superiority over current Silero on lecture rooms. WebRTC VAD is a useful tiny baseline, not the preferred far-field default.

**Proposed lecture starting parameters—not universal optima:** threshold **0.45–0.5**, minimum speech **150–250ms**, silence **500–800ms**, padding **200–300ms**, maximum decoding chunk **20–30s**. Tune for **missed quiet speech**, not merely false triggers. Current vox’s hard gate should not decide what audio survives.

## D. Speaker diarization

Sherpa provides **offline** segmentation → embeddings → clustering, including **pyannote segmentation 3.0**, **3D-Speaker**, and **NeMo TitaNet** embeddings.

Its [examples](https://k2-fsa.github.io/sherpa/onnx/speaker-diarization/models.html) report approximately **RTF 0.11–0.30** for pyannote combinations on a 57-second clip; hardware is unspecified, so these are not capacity forecasts.

Use it **after recording**, optional by default. Supply speaker count when known; otherwise validate clustering thresholds. Hours-long recordings require memory measurements and potentially blockwise processing with global speaker reconciliation. Online embedding matching is not equivalent to verified online diarization. Keep anonymous labels, support revisions, and flag overlap rather than inventing speaker identity.

## E. Transcript structure and blog preparation

Sherpa offers [English Edge-Punct-Casing and bilingual CT-Transformer](https://k2-fsa.github.io/sherpa/onnx/punctuation/pretrained_models.html). Prefer the English model for English-only restoration; the documented bilingual example emits Chinese punctuation in English text. Avoid repunctuating already punctuated Parakeet output indiscriminately.

**Vox should own:**

- Immutable raw ASR plus separately versioned cleaned text.
- Word/segment timestamps, speaker labels, confidence/provenance.
- Sentence splitting and provisional paragraphs using punctuation, pauses and speaker changes.
- Searchable excerpts referencing segment IDs and recording ranges.

**The calling LLM should own:** topic interpretation, chapter titles, arguments, summaries and blog prose.

TextTiling’s lexical-cohesion idea now has supervised/embedding successors. [TextSeg](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.09337) provides a supervised foundation; [Smart Chaptering/YTSEG, 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.eacl-long.25/) directly studies transcript segmentation and title generation. These justify optional topic boundaries—not authoritative chapters.

For quotes, require source segment IDs and literal text spans; distinguish verbatim transcription from editorial cleanup.

## F. Room-audio DSP

**The resampler needs correction before adding denoising.** In `src/audio.rs:441`, the interpolation kernel is not scaled for downsampling. At 48→16kHz it is essentially decimation without the necessary low-pass filter. Processing callbacks independently also loses filter history/fractional phase.

Use a stateful anti-aliased resampler such as [rubato](https://github.com/HEnquist/rubato), outside an allocation-heavy callback.

Other changes:

- **HPF:** retain configurable rumble/DC filtering, but try **70–100Hz**, not mandatory 200Hz. Measure WER; filtering cannot remove room reverberation.
- **Gate:** disable destructive zeroing in archival capture. It can remove quiet consonants and distant questions.
- **Gain:** replace unrestricted segment peak boosting with bounded gain and slow attack/release if needed. AGC can amplify noise; raw audio must remain available.
- **Noise suppression:** A/B test [sherpa GTCRN](https://k2-fsa.github.io/sherpa/onnx/c-api/html/speech_enhancement.html) first for integration simplicity. [RNNoise](https://gitlab.xiph.org/xiph/rnnoise) is a mature lightweight alternative; [DeepFilterNet](https://github.com/Rikorose/DeepFilterNet) offers Rust components and full-band 48kHz enhancement. Perceptual improvement does not guarantee lower WER.
- **Loudness:** [EBU R128](https://tech.ebu.ch/publications/r128) specifies **−23 LUFS integrated** programme loudness. Useful for listening exports, not an ASR requirement or per-chunk normalization target.

A closer microphone generally beats increasingly aggressive restoration.

## G. TTS

**Keep Kokoro.** Vox already uses sherpa’s multilingual v1.1 export; distinguish that artifact from [upstream Kokoro-82M v1.0](https://huggingface.co/hexgrad/Kokoro-82M) and the separate v1.1-zh checkpoint.

[KittenTTS](https://github.com/KittenML/KittenTTS) now offers 15M/40M/80M models; sherpa documents v0.8 exports. It merits an optional low-memory preset, not an assumed quality upgrade. Piper/VITS, Matcha and other [sherpa TTS backends](https://k2-fsa.github.io/sherpa/onnx/tts/pretrained_models/index.html) expand voice/language choices but do not solve lecture requirements. Check individual voice licenses.

### Recommended harness contract

Provide durable `recording_start/stop/status/cancel` and `transcript_segments(cursor, limit)`, independent of protocol support.

Return structured IDs, state, captured duration, decoded-through time, backlog, dropped frames and artifact URIs. Distinguish **stop-and-finalize** from **cancel-processing**; neither should delete audio.

Use [progress notifications](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2025-11-25/basic/utilities/progress) when supported; unknown total is legitimate. Notifications are not persistence. Negotiate the [Tasks extension](https://tasks.extensions.modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/draft/tasks), retaining polling fallback.

Expose resources as bounded transcript pages/audio ranges. Resource-list pagination does **not** automatically paginate a giant resource body. Use cursors plus revision/snapshot IDs.

## I. Testing and profiling

Extend existing Criterion/DSP-quality work with:

- **[Property tests](https://github.com/proptest-rs/proptest):** finite outputs, valid-rate handling, sample-count conservation, same-rate identity, chunked/batch equivalence after delay compensation, DC/passband response and above-Nyquist rejection.
- Gate energy must not increase; normalization should be idempotent. **Do not assert every filter preserves energy or stays within ±1:** ringing/overshoot needs explicit tolerances.
- Licensed golden fixtures: silence, quiet speech, boundary-crossing words, noise, clipping, stereo cancellation and long uninterrupted speech.
- Model-gated WER regressions using frozen references/normalization; measure insertions on silence, timestamp error and boundary omissions separately.
- Multi-hour soak and fault tests: stalled decoding, microphone removal, disk-full, process kill, restart and final-tail recovery.

Use [Criterion](https://criterion-rs.github.io/book/) for kernels and [samply](https://github.com/mstange/samply)/[flamegraph](https://github.com/flamegraph-rs/flamegraph) for whole pipelines. Vox strips symbols and disables dev debug information; profiling needs a symbol-bearing build. Trace worker stages and record RTF, p95 latency, RSS, queue depth and callback overruns; keep logging/allocations off the real-time callback.
